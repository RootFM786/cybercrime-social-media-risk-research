# Risk Findings

## Core Finding: Awareness–Action Gap

The survey showed a notable difference between **knowing cybercrime is a risk** and **taking proactive action to reduce that risk**.

Of the 12 businesses surveyed:

- respondents generally recognised common cybercrime types
- most used social media frequently
- respondents believed a cyber incident could seriously affect their organisation
- only 2 reported having taken proactive steps to avoid becoming a victim

## Risk Interpretation

### 1. Public Information Exposure

Frequent use of social media increases the amount of public information available about a business, its staff, relationships and activities.

That information can make social-engineering attempts more convincing.

### 2. Human Behaviour

The report identified examples where users may reveal useful personal information through social-media interactions.

This creates opportunities for:

- phishing
- impersonation
- account-recovery abuse
- targeted social engineering

### 3. Operational Dependency

Participants described social media, CRM systems and online services as important to normal business operations.

This means account takeover, ransomware or service disruption could have consequences beyond simple data loss.

### 4. Limited Defensive Action

The most important security observation is that risk awareness did not necessarily result in:

- training
- formal security planning
- specialist support
- structured recovery preparation

## Security Takeaway

Risk is created by the combination of:

```text
Exposure + Human Behaviour + Weak Controls + Business Dependency
```

That combination is more useful to a security analyst than treating social media itself as the threat.
