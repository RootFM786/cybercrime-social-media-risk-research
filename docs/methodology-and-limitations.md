# Methodology and Limitations

## Research Design

The project used a mixed-method survey containing both quantitative and qualitative questions.

Participants were 12 purposely selected UK small-business owners in the retail and service sectors who used at least two major social-media platforms.

The survey covered:

- business size
- social-media usage
- frequency of use
- awareness of cybercrime types
- previous victimisation
- defensive measures
- perceived impact of cybercrime
- examples of suspicious social-media activity

## Why Mixed Methods Were Useful

Quantitative responses helped identify patterns, while open-ended responses provided context about how business owners understood cyber risk and potential impact.

## Limitations

The original report recognised several important limitations:

- the sample size was small
- participants were purposefully selected rather than randomly sampled
- results therefore have limited generalisability
- no respondent reported being a known victim of cybercrime
- the study did not fully explore why awareness failed to translate into proactive action

## Current Reflection

If repeated today, I would add questions about:

- MFA adoption
- password-manager use
- email-security controls
- endpoint protection
- backup and recovery testing
- cybersecurity training frequency
- previous phishing attempts and near misses
- third-party IT/security support

This would make the study more useful for identifying specific control gaps rather than only measuring awareness.
